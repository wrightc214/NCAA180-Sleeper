NCAA 180 is a dynasty fantasy football super league on Sleeper: 180 teams, each named for a college program, in 15 conferences of 12. Each conference runs as its own Sleeper league through the regular season and its championship game; then all 180 teams meet in one shared postseason — a 32-team playoff, bowl games and the NIT.

The season

  1. Weeks 1–11 Regular season, inside each conference.
  2. Week 12 Conference championship games: the two division winners meet.
  3. Weeks 13–17 Postseason: playoff, bowls and the NIT.

Trade deadline: the end of Monday Night Football in week 10.

Rosters & rules from the league bylaws

  • Format: dynasty, PPR scoring.
  • Starting lineup: QB · 2 RB · 3 WR · TE · 2 FLEX · K · DEF; 30 bench spots.
  • Position limits: QB 10 · RB 25 · WR 25 · TE 15 · K 6 · DEF 6.
  • Common scoring: conferences have used different scoring settings over the years, so the site rescores every game on one standard scheme to compare across conferences. From 2026, results are decided on it; a † marks a game where Sleeper's own number differs.
  • No waiver wire (except briefly after rookie drafts): free agents are first come, first served.
  • Trades can be vetoed by the conference manager or by a 7 of 10 vote (the two teams in the trade don't vote; 6 of 9 for three-team trades, and so on).
  • Draft picks can be traded two seasons out; the season "resets" after each rookie draft.
  • No tanking by lineup: you must start a real lineup. To tank, trade players away. The conference manager can edit lineups, including after the fact.

Postseason

Playoff

32 teams: the 15 conference champions (championship-game winners) plus the 17 best remaining teams by regular-season record (win %, then points for, then points against). The ranking committee seeds the field; the bracket is fixed (1 v 32, 2 v 31 …), with no reseeding and no byes. The Elite 8 and Final Four are hosted by the six playoff bowls on a yearly rotation, and the National Championship follows the real title game's site.

Bowls

Every non-playoff team with 6+ wins goes bowling; if the count is odd, the best 5-win team is added. 36 bowls in four tiers, weakest to strongest: Opening Bowls, Showcase Bowls, Marquee Bowls, Prestige Bowls. Prestige Bowls take the top of the bowl order (poll rank, with each extra win worth 25 poll spots); Marquee Bowls give each conference a seat. Bowl winners earn a compensatory 3rd-round pick (playoff games don't count).

NIT

Everyone else. Group stages of four (seeded by pots from the poll), with each group winner plus the best-scoring runners-up ("lucky dogs") advancing to 28 teams, then 7 groups and 1 more lucky dog to an 8-team bracket: quarterfinals, semifinals and the Stagg Bowl. The NIT winner earns a compensatory 4th-round pick.

Rookie draft order

Conference champions pick last in their conference's rookie draft and championship-game losers pick 11th; everyone else by record.
