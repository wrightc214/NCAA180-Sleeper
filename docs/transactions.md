# Transactions dashboard

The Transactions page is configured as hidden-dev. It shows completed trades, weekly player movement, and season summaries from the repository's Sleeper data. Trade grades measure the market value each team received, separately for dynasty (Rebuild) and rest-of-season market value (Contender). They are not Team Fit grades.

## Implemented rules

- Use the latest historical FantasyCalc player and pick snapshots dated on or before the trade's UTC date. Never substitute a later snapshot or today's values. Missing history and unresolved players stay ungraded. A recorded zero is a valid observation.
- An identified QB, RB, WR, TE, or FB absent from an otherwise valid player snapshot is worth zero. An identified unlisted kicker or D/ST instead gets the upcoming draft's historical **4.12** estimate in both grades. K and D/ST are required positions without a free-agent acquisition path in NCAA 180. The proxy is labeled on the card, included in point totals, and excluded from the consolidation calculation. If 4.12 cannot be estimated, withhold affected grades. The NCAA 180 K/DST pool is fully rostered, undrafted rookie kickers are claimed quickly, and a weak rookie kicker usually costs about a fourth. This market baseline is not itself a measurement of a team's lineup need.
- Traded rookie picks count in Rebuild, but are worth zero in Contender. When both sides have zero measured value, show **No measurable value**, not Even.
- Dynasty penalizes the spread of value across an incoming package. For eligible trade-date values `v`, subtract `0.60 × (sum(v) − sqrt(sum(v²)))` from raw value. K/DST proxy points count in raw value but not in the spread formula. A single asset is not penalized, and adding a nonnegative asset never lowers the package score. The displayed edge is the difference between the two penalties and is already included. This replaces the former experimental KTC-style balancing credit. Contender uses raw rest-of-season totals.
- **Market grade display caps** use both sides’ combined raw points, including K/DST proxies, as a percentage of the corresponding leader in the full trade-date snapshot. Rebuild uses dynasty values and Contender uses redraft values. The default maximum ranges are C−/C+ below 10%, D+/B− from 10% to under 20%, D/B from 20% to under 30%, D−/B+ from 30% to under 40%, and no cap at or above 40%. Only displayed letters change. Even, ungraded results, values, surplus, Team Fit and Blockbuster are unchanged. Set `transactions.rebuild_grade_caps` or `transactions.contender_grade_caps` to `[]` to disable that category’s caps.
- Only the team with the positive adjusted dynasty difference gets a surplus badge. Equivalents use the upcoming rookie draft **as of the trade**, and the badge omits the year. Asset listings still show each traded pick's actual year. At or above 4.12, show the nearest single pick through the early-first cap, then start with 1.01 and match the remaining value to a different slot above the cap when slot estimates are available. Below 4.12, find the closest estimated same-draft upgrade anchored at 4.12. If no upgrade is within 25% of the actual surplus, show points only. Estimated slots are marked approximate.
- **Blockbuster** marks a big deal with a headliner, not fairness or a winner. Both cutoffs use the most valuable player in the **full trade-date FantasyCalc snapshot**, even if that player was not traded. The combined raw dynasty market value of both sides must reach **1.40×** that benchmark, and at least one actual traded player must reach **60%** of it individually. Picks count toward the combined total but cannot be the headliner. Neither the spread penalty nor K/DST proxy points count toward either test. Incomplete dynasty valuations are ineligible. The repo owner can change both cutoffs, the grade bands, the dynasty concentration rate, the K/DST proxy slot and the surplus swap tolerance in `config/transactions.json` under `transactions`. Changing the proxy slot also changes the swap anchor.
  - With an 11,000-point market leader, the thresholds are 15,400 combined and 6,600 for one player. A 1,000-point player for two 750-point players fails both; a 17,000-point pile with no qualifying player fails the second. A star plus filler can still qualify. Review real examples before adding another rule.

## Task 5.11 owner configuration

- `config/transactions.json` → `transactions` controls the grade bands, consolidation method (`asset-concentration`) and dynasty rate (`0.60`), Blockbuster cutoffs, proxy and small-swap settings, draft field size and interpolation anchors, movement types, and visible feature labels. A missing file, section, or key keeps the NCAA 180 defaults. The report generator embeds the resolved values in the static page. To disable consolidation and grade raw totals, set `consolidation.method` to `none`.
- `config/transactions.json` → `fantasycalc` controls the public API endpoint, scoring format, timeout and minimum acceptable response size. Changing the format affects new snapshots only. Do not mix formats in historical comparisons without checking comparability.
- Transaction cards, both week selectors, and the summary share the site’s schedule and week-label settings via `league_config.week_label()`. Week filter values stay numeric.
- `config/nav.json` owns the menu label, link and visibility. The Transactions entry explicitly sets `hidden-dev`. Like any tab, a complete entry without a state defaults to `visible`. Removing the entry omits its link and does not block the direct URL. Dev visibility is not access control. Shared site colour tokens and CSV team colours remain the sources for styling.

Trade cards use `config/league.json` → `position_colors` for the small player-position badges. The palette matches the Scoreboard (QB pink, RB mint, WR blue, TE apricot, K lavender, DEF peach), with dark badge text. Player names and picks stay neutral. Unconfigured positions get a neutral badge.

## Team Fit

Trade cards emphasize Team Fit and show market grades as smaller supporting figures. Their colors use the shared theme scale, pulled toward neutral by each team's relevance weight (win-now for Contender, one minus win-now for Rebuild), and by the `transactions.market_grade_color_strength` setting (default `0.5`). Without available Fit, market letters are neutral. Letters, scores and points are unaffected.

Team Fit is a separate, experimental team-goal grade. It compares trade-date best lineup changes and future value versus holding against the team's archived situation. Dated data snapshots establish which inputs were available and corroborate pre- and post-trade ownership. The comparison is a trade-time judgment, not a report of what a player did later. An unverified or incomplete trade is shown as `-`, not Even. Age decline uses immutable birth dates, position-specific onset and steep decline rates, and separate one- and two-year horizons. Current market price and Blockbuster eligibility are unchanged. Missing player IDs count as zero for Team Fit. Projected wins and odds changes remain unavailable.

Owner controls live in `config/transactions.json` under `team_fit`. These are review-model settings, not direct output from FantasyCalc. Routine builds need no Git history. The weekly job records the capture window and appends a data snapshot under `data/trade-fit/snapshots/` after the roster, pick, transaction, market and context inputs run. A capture must end before acceptance to supply pre-trade context, prices or odds. A later capture must start after acceptance and may only validate ownership, not supply market values. Newly pulled completed transactions use Sleeper's status-update timestamp, with creation as a conservative fallback when unknown.

Published Team Fit results live in `data/trade-fit/grades.jsonl`, keyed by season, league, transaction and recipient roster. The ledger stores the model and settings fingerprint, cutoff, source identifiers, score and supporting inputs. Routine runs append revision 1 for new or previously ungraded sides only. A deliberate correction appends the next revision with a `correction_reason` and uses only evidence available at the original cutoff. A later injury or result never rewrites the grade. A pending observation and permanently missing evidence both render a hyphen, with distinct accessible labels when waiting for a later snapshot.

The one-time `python3 scripts/trade_fit_update.py --backfill-from-git` command requires full history. It exports the verified results and a starter observation. Older provenance can link to archived commits, but no reader needs those commits. A fresh league without imported snapshots starts collecting evidence with its first weekly run.

## Open work

1. **“Why this trade makes sense” narrative — priority follow-up.** Write a short explanation for each team, grounded in trade-date evidence. Explain what the team gains, what it gives up, and what makes the move reasonable or questionable for its competitive window. Distinguish measured market price from lineup value, flag inferred intent, and do not invent a manager's motive. Bass illustrates the difference between a league-level kicker proxy and the strategic value of filling an actual vacancy. If context is missing, say what can be established from the assets without pretending to know the team's plan.
2. **Trade-time team tags (follow-up).** Explore Rebuilder, Bowl Aspirant, Championship Contender and other clearly defined tags using roster value, record and manager activity known before each trade. Show evidence and uncertainty, then test whether tags should select goal-specific formulas. Do not turn a team's eventual result or current tags into historical intent.
3. **Projected ROS wins and before/after odds.** Validate a historical lineup-aware counterfactual against known baseline forecasts. Account for schedule, injuries and byes known at the time, replacement starters, and playoff and draft-order rules. Show title/playoff odds for contenders and bowl/1.01 odds for rebuilding teams only when supported. Until validated, leave those figures unavailable.
4. **Revisit dynasty calibration with more data.** The 60% rate was chosen after fitting mean absolute adjusted gaps on 34 dated trades, treating them as even for calibration only. It produced an 879-point average gap versus 996 with raw totals and 872 at the sample optimum (53.2%). This is not evidence that every trade was even.

5. **Trade explorer (follow-up PR).** Default to newest first. Keep the current conference, week and team filters and allow the new controls to work in combination:
   - **Player-name search.** As the user types, show only trades containing a matching player on either side. Match full or partial names case-insensitively, not team names, pick labels or report notes.
   - **Grade sort and filters.** Choose Team Fit, Rebuild or Contender. Use the selected team's grade when a team is selected. With no team selected, best-first ranks by the higher of the two grades and worst-first ranks by the lower. For a minimum-grade filter, match either side or require both sides. Sort trades without a comparable grade last.
   - **Total value and gap.** Sort or filter by combined raw dynasty value. Offer a “Most lopsided” sort by absolute adjusted dynasty gap or absolute raw redraft gap, with the basis labeled. Keep unavailable values distinct from zero.
   - **Blockbusters only.** Filter on the existing tag. Add projected wins added to the explorer only after that measure is validated.
6. **One-year trade outcome grade (later PR).** At one year after acceptance, grade each team's realized return using evidence through that anniversary. Show this alongside the original trade-time grade without recalculating or replacing it. A later injury may affect the outcome grade, not the trade-time judgment.
